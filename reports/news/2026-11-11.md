<b>═ 🔥 TOP STORY ═</b>
Nordic announced the nRF71 Series on 3 Nov 2026, starting with the nRF7120: a single SoC with dual-band Wi-Fi 6, a 2.4 GHz multiprotocol radio for Bluetooth LE and Thread, and a 256 MHz Arm Cortex-M33 application core. The nRF7120E adds the 6 GHz band (Wi-Fi 6E) and Nordic's Axon NPU. Nordic's product page lists 14 µA at 2.4 GHz and 8 µA at 5 GHz in connected standby at DTIM10 (3.6 V), and Nordic claims up to 20x lower standby power than its nRF70 companion chips; the test conditions behind the 20x figure are not published. Parts are sampling to selected customers, with broad availability not yet dated. Why it matters: this is a direct competitor to the ESP32-S3 class for battery-powered Wi-Fi + BLE products like the P2CAM camera, with Zephyr / nRF Connect SDK instead of ESP-IDF.

<b>1. Post: Capability unlock — Wi-Fi standby at 14 µA</b>
Nordic's new nRF7120 lists 14 µA in Wi-Fi connected standby at DTIM10.

For years the rule for battery IoT was simple: BLE or Thread for the radio, Wi-Fi only if you have mains power or a big battery. The nRF71 puts Wi-Fi 6, BLE and Thread on one die with a Cortex-M33, and Nordic claims up to 20x lower standby than its previous Wi-Fi companion chips.

The detail that matters is DTIM10. Standby current depends on how often the radio wakes for beacons, so the real figure in your product depends on your AP settings and latency budget, not just the datasheet.

What DTIM interval would you accept in a battery Wi-Fi device?

<b>2. Post: Security wake-up — the unauthenticated dev interface</b>
A CVSS 10.0 bug, an advisory from August, and active exploitation at the end of October.

CVE-2026-58115 affects Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices with Node-RED installed: the HTTP interface had no authentication, and Node-RED has built-in nodes that execute system commands. VulnCheck's honeypots logged 16 attempts in three days to drop a Linux bot.

The engineering lesson is not about Node-RED. Any convenience interface that can change device behaviour, whether a web editor, a BLE provisioning service or a debug shell, is a remote code path unless it is authenticated.

Which interface on your device would you be least comfortable seeing on Shodan?

<b>3. Post: Integration leap — DSP and NPU on a Cortex-M7</b>
ST's new STM32H7P puts a hardware signal processor and an 85 GOPS NPU next to a 520 MHz Cortex-M7.

ST says the signal processor runs FFTs up to 5x faster than software on the M7, and that the NPU ran its solar-inverter arc-fault model 40x faster than software.

That combination matches a very common pipeline: transform the signal, then classify it. Until now you usually chose between a DSP-friendly MCU and an AI-friendly one. Offloading both stages frees the M7 for control loops, which is exactly what power and motor-control designs need.

Would you move FFT pre-processing into hardware, or keep it in CMSIS-DSP for portability?

<b>4. Post: Regulatory shift — standards that exist but don't count yet</b>
23 European industry associations are asking the Commission to do one thing: cite the EN IEC 62443 standards in the Official Journal once CENELEC submits them.

Under the Cyber Resilience Act, a harmonised standard only gives presumption of conformity after citation. Until then, firmware teams can follow IEC 62443 and still have to argue compliance on their own.

Trackers show the related OT standards were still drafts in October, while CRA reporting duties have applied since September 2026.

If you build connected products for the EU, are you designing to IEC 62443 now or waiting for the citation?

<b>5. Post: Physics-meets-architecture — harvest or charge, one PCB</b>
e-peas announced PMICs that handle both ambient energy harvesting and 5 V charging from a single PCB design.

The AEM11920 and AEM11921 combine a boost converter for the harvester, a buck output for the load and a 5 V charger. Samples are due in December.

The insight: a harvesting product usually fails on the worst-case week, not the average day. Adding a 5 V charge path lets you size the harvester for typical light and fall back to USB for the rest, instead of oversizing the solar cell to cover every dark winter.

Would you design battery-free first, or rechargeable with harvesting as a top-up?
