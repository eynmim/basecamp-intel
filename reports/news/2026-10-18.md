<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — The field you forgot to sign</b>
A signature check that passes when one property is missing is not a signature check.

CVE-2026-46728 affects U-Boot before 2026.04. In FIT image verification, omitting the hashed-nodes property let unauthorised kernels, device trees or ramdisks load.

The insight: verified boot fails at the edges of the format, not in the cryptography. What the verifier treats as optional is what an attacker controls.

Does your bootloader reject images by default, or accept what it cannot check?

https://aviatrix.ai/threat-research-center/new-u-boot-flaws-could-enable-stealthy-firmware-attacks-2026/

<b>2. Post: Tooling unlock — A minor SDK bump that is not minor</b>
ESP-IDF v6.1 is called "mostly compatible" with v6.0, yet its pre-releases listed breaking API and component changes.

It also previews support for ESP32-S31, so one toolchain now spans a new RISC-V part and the chips already in products.

The practical lesson: "mostly compatible" is a promise about your app code, not your component manager, your sdkconfig or your CI cache. I would branch, build, and diff the config before trusting it.

How long does your team wait before adopting a new SDK minor?

<b>3. Post: Regulatory shift — Disclosure is now part of the job</b>
Since 11 September, EU manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities in their products.

That is the CRA reporting duty. The rest of the regulation applies from 11 December 2027, so the process arrives before the full compliance work.

For firmware engineers this makes vulnerability handling a design input: an SBOM, a way to push a patch, and a contact who can answer in hours.

Could your team find every device running a given library version today?

https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act

<b>4. Post: Capability unlock — An NPU on a microcontroller</b>
TI claims up to 90x lower latency and 120x lower energy per inference with an NPU on the MCU.

Those are vendor numbers from the March MSPM0G5187 and AM13Ex announcement, so treat them as an upper bound against a no-accelerator baseline.

If NPUs spread across MCU portfolios, a model becomes a normal firmware dependency and the questions turn to memory layout and power budgets.

Would you design around a vendor NPU or stay portable?

<b>5. Post: Integration leap — Matter still runs on BLE</b>
Every Thread-based Matter device must also carry Bluetooth LE, because that is how a new device joins the network.

Matter moves data over Thread or Wi-Fi, but commissioning goes through BLE. The stack you ship is really three radios' worth of firmware behaving as one.

For an engineer that means BLE provisioning bugs become smart-home onboarding bugs, which users blame on the product, not the protocol.

Where does your commissioning flow fail most often?
