<b>═ 🔥 TOP STORY ═</b>
Binarly reported six U-Boot CVEs in FIT image signature verification, including two remote-code-execution bugs, that can break secure boot on more than 50 firmware releases. Because verified boot trusts the FIT signature path, a flaw there lets unsigned code run at the earliest stage of boot. Devices from vendors that no longer support older models may never receive a patch. Why it matters: any Linux-class board you ship or study that uses U-Boot verified boot inherits this class of bug. Exact CVE IDs and dates should be checked against Binarly's advisory; the link below is a secondary report.

<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — the signature check is the attack surface</b>
Six U-Boot CVEs, two of them remote code execution, all in one place: FIT signature verification.

Binarly's report says they break secure boot across 50+ firmware releases. The check meant to prove the image is trusted is the part that fails.

The lesson I take: verified boot is only as strong as the parser in front of the signature. Complexity before verification is attack surface, and unsupported devices may never get a fix.

How do you audit what runs before your signature check?

https://dailysecurityreview.com/?p=41960

<b>2. Post: Tooling unlock — TI moves to Zephyr</b>
TI now supports Zephyr across its MCU, MPU and wireless lines.

The announcement (1 Oct 2026) covers parts from Cortex-M0 up to Cortex-M33, with validated releases and migration guidance for products with lifecycles beyond 10 years.

The engineering point: a vendor SDK becoming a Zephyr port makes RTOS and driver code more portable across silicon. Not every TI part is covered, so check your exact device first.

Would you move a product from a vendor SDK to Zephyr?

<b>3. Post: Regulatory shift — CRA reporting is already live</b>
The EU Cyber Resilience Act's reporting obligations began on 11 September 2026. The main obligations follow on 11 December 2027.

For firmware teams this is not abstract. Vulnerability handling, update paths and traceable components become part of the product, not an afterthought.

OTA, secure boot and a disclosure process are now design requirements. Is your team ready to report a vulnerability within the required window?
