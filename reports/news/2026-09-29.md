<b>═ 🔥 TOP STORY ═</b>
The EU Cyber Resilience Act's reporting obligations have applied since 11 September 2026: manufacturers of products with digital elements must now report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA/CSIRTs. Full CRA requirements (vulnerability handling, free security updates, SBOM) follow in 2027, while EN 18031-1/-2/-3 have been mandatory for radio equipment under the RED delegated act since 1 August 2025. For anyone shipping ESP32/STM32 connected products in the EU, firmware update pipelines and SBOM tooling are now a compliance topic, not a nice-to-have. Why it matters: security engineering skills (secure boot, OTA, SBOM, PSIRT process) are becoming a hiring filter for EU embedded roles.

<b>1. Post: Regulatory shift — the CRA clock is already ticking</b>
Since 11 September, EU firmware teams have a legal duty to report exploited vulnerabilities.

The Cyber Resilience Act's reporting obligations now apply, well before the full requirements land in 2027. That includes vulnerability handling, free security updates and an SBOM.

The engineering insight: this isn't a paperwork problem. You can't report what you can't detect, and you can't patch what you can't OTA. Update pipeline, signed images and a component inventory are now product features.

For those shipping ESP32 or STM32 products in the EU: is your OTA path ready for a mandatory patch?

<b>3. Post: Capability unlock — NPUs arrive at MCU scale</b>
120x less energy per inference is the number TI puts on its new MCU NPU.

TI's TinyEngine NPU shows up in the MSPM0G5187 and AM13Ex families, with a claimed up to 90x lower latency. On the ST side, the STM32N6 pairs an 800 MHz Cortex-M55 with a 600 GOPS accelerator.

The insight: these are vendor claims against CPU-only baselines, so the real question is model support and toolchain maturity, not peak numbers. A fast NPU without a good quantization flow is dead silicon.

<b>4. Post: Tooling unlock — Zephyr SDK 1.0</b>
Zephyr 4.4 quietly changed the build story: Zephyr SDK 1.0.

It brings an upgraded GNU toolchain, experimental Clang/LLVM support, and QEMU and OpenOCD host tools across platforms. C17 is now the default minimum standard.

For CI this matters more than any new driver. A reproducible toolchain plus QEMU means firmware tests can run without hardware on the bench.

The release also adds Wi-Fi P2P and WireGuard, but the SDK is the part I'd read first.

<b>5. Post: Security wake-up — read your chip's own vulnerability page</b>
Your MCU vendor probably already published its known weaknesses.

Espressif's ESP-IDF docs list per-chip vulnerabilities, including Bluetooth issues such as CVE-2020-26555 to 26560, with the status for each chip, ESP32-S3 included.

The insight: many BLE flaws are spec-level, not implementation bugs. Updating the SDK won't fix what the specification allows, so pairing mode and security settings become your responsibility.

When did you last audit your product's BLE pairing configuration against a known-issues list?
