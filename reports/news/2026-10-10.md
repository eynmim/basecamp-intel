<b>═ 🔥 TOP STORY ═</b>
The CRA harmonised standards are running late. The original standardisation request put the horizontal and vulnerability-handling drafts at 30 August 2026 and the product-specific drafts at 30 October 2026. A Commission draft amendment from early July proposes moving them to 31 October and 31 December 2026. It is not yet in the Official Journal, and as of 30 July no CRA harmonised standard has been cited for any product category. The reporting duty that started on 11 September is unaffected. Why it matters: until the standards are cited, firmware teams build against drafts such as prEN 40000-1-3, so secure update and vulnerability handling practices matter more than a checklist.

<b>1. Post: Regulatory shift — the standards behind the CRA are late</b>
The law is live, but the standards that define how to comply are not finished.

The CRA reporting duty started on 11 September. The harmonised standards were due from August, and a draft amendment proposes pushing them back by two months. As of late July none was cited in the Official Journal.

For firmware teams this means building against drafts such as prEN 40000-1-3 for vulnerability handling. Waiting for the final text is not an option when the reporting duty already applies.

Are you building to the drafts, or waiting?

An MCU with a neural accelerator on board now costs less than a dollar in volume.

<b>3. Post: Security wake-up — an RTOS CVE with a public PoC</b>
A 13-byte leak is small, but where it sits is the point.

CVE-2026-10672 is an out-of-bounds read in Zephyr's LwM2M firmware-update component, reachable through crafted CoAP requests. It affects 3.0.0 through 4.4.0, so most pinned projects are inside the range.

The update path is the code that has to accept untrusted input before anything is verified, which is why bugs there matter more than their byte count. I would check the official record, since the PoC report comes from an aggregator.

Does your update client parse anything before it authenticates it?

<b>5. Post: Integration leap — EU law meets the build system</b>
Reporting an exploited vulnerability starts with knowing what is in your build.

Under the CRA, manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities, and that means tracing every third-party component in every shipped image.

I treat an SBOM as part of CI now, generated per build, not as paperwork at release time. For ESP-IDF and Zephyr projects that is a small script and a real answer when someone asks what a given image contains.
