A cluster of FatFs CVEs (CVE-2026-6682 through CVE-2026-6687) is reported to affect Espressif ESP-IDF and STMicroelectronics STM32Cube middleware, which both bundle the FatFs library. The reported bugs include a FAT32 mount integer overflow, an exFAT volume-label buffer overflow and an exFAT divide-by-zero. <b>1. Post: Security wake-up — one filesystem library, two vendors</b>
A single FAT parser bug can sit under both ESP32 and STM32 firmware.

Six CVEs, CVE-2026-6682 through CVE-2026-6687, are reported against FatFs, which both Espressif's ESP-IDF and ST's STM32Cube middleware bundle. They cover a FAT32 mount integer overflow, an exFAT volume-label buffer overflow and a divide-by-zero.

The insight is the attack path: the attacker needs no network, only a crafted SD card or USB stick. Any device that auto-mounts removable media treats the filesystem image as trusted input.

I now review filesystem mounts the way I review a network parser.

Where in your product does a file system image cross a trust boundary?

<b>2. Post: Capability unlock — an NPU next to a Cortex-M0+</b>
An NPU is now showing up beside the smallest Arm core.

TI's MSPM0G5187 pairs its TinyEngine NPU with a Cortex-M0+. TI claims up to 90x lower latency and over 120x lower energy per inference than MCUs without an accelerator. Those are vendor numbers.

The engineering takeaway: the M0+ no longer has to run the model, only orchestrate it. Inference becomes a peripheral job, like a DMA transfer, which changes how you budget power and RAM.

The catch is yet another vendor toolchain next to ST's and Arm's.

Would you pick an MCU for its NPU toolchain before its CPU?

<b>3. Post: Integration leap — a bigger sibling to the ESP32-S3</b>
Gigabit Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and 802.15.4 on one ESP32.

The interesting bit is the radio mix. With 802.15.4 next to Wi-Fi and BLE, a single chip can sit as a Thread or Matter node and a wired gateway at once, which used to mean two chips.

<b>4. Post: Regulatory shift — the standard that doesn't exist yet</b>
The EU Cyber Resilience Act is live, but no harmonised standard backs it.

As of mid-2026 none has been cited in the Official Journal. The Article 27 presumption of conformity therefore isn't available yet, and reporting obligations already apply.

For firmware teams that means arguing compliance from the essential requirements directly, with evidence: SBOMs, signed updates, a vulnerability intake process.

IEC 62443 and EN 18031 are the closest guides today, and the 62443 alignment work is still underway.

I'm building that evidence trail into my own repos now, not later.

How are you documenting compliance before the standards land?

<b>5. Post: Tooling unlock — ESPHome drops the PlatformIO layer</b>
ESPHome now builds ESP32 firmware with native ESP-IDF by default.

With ESPHome 2026.7, the esp32 platform no longer goes through PlatformIO unless you set it explicitly. Existing configs that choose the PlatformIO toolchain build exactly as before.

The practical gain is one fewer abstraction between your config and the SDK, so errors point at ESP-IDF directly and new releases like v6.1 are easier to adopt.

Fewer layers also means fewer places for a pinned version to drift.
