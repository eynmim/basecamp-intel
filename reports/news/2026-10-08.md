<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — ECDSA secure boot switched off</b>
Espressif just disabled a secure boot scheme in its own SDK.

ESP-IDF v5.2.8, v5.3.6, v5.5.5 and v6.1 all disable ECDSA Secure Boot V2 on ESP32-H2, ESP32-C5 and ESP32-P4, citing a vulnerability in the ECDSA-based flow.

The part that stuck with me: the fix is to remove the option, not patch it. If your product signed with ECDSA on one of these chips, the root of trust is the thing to re-audit, and key choice is now a lifecycle decision, not a build flag.

Which signing scheme do you ship, and could you change it in the field?

<b>2. Post: Capability unlock — a 13-byte leak in a firmware updater</b>
13 bytes is all it takes to make an updater a liability.

An out-of-bounds read in Zephyr's LwM2M firmware update component (CVE-2026-10672) affects versions 3.0.0 through 4.4.0 and can expose up to 13 bytes of memory, with a public proof of concept reported.

Thirteen bytes sounds harmless. But the update path is the one component that must be reachable from outside and trusted from inside, so even a small leak there can help with defeating memory layout assumptions.

Do you threat-model your OTA client as hostile-input code?

<b>3. Post: Tooling unlock — a release train with an end date</b>
Two of my toolchains just got an expiry date.

ESP-IDF 5.2 ended at v5.2.8, and Zephyr 4.3 reaches end of life on 15 October. Zephyr 4.5 is expected this month on its six-month cadence.

The engineering point: a firmware product outlives its SDK branch. Every device you ship needs a plan for moving branches, because security fixes only flow to the lines that are still alive, and the NimBLE fixes in 5.2.8 are a good example.

Joint Fabric is the Matter feature I would read first.

Matter 1.6 (17 June 2026) adds NFC-based setup, Joint Fabric so multiple ecosystems can co-administer a device, and Thermostat Suggestions.

For firmware engineers, the interesting part is that one device may now answer to several administrators. That changes how you think about credentials, commissioning flow and what a factory reset really means.

<b>5. Post: Regulatory shift — CRA makes secure boot a requirement</b>
By December 2027, secure boot is not a differentiator in the EU, it is paperwork.

The Cyber Resilience Act reaches full compliance on 11 December 2027, and EN 18031 already applies under RED. Signed updates, vulnerability handling and a secure boot chain become product requirements.

The ECDSA story in ESP-IDF shows why this is hard: even a correct design can be disabled upstream and you still have to explain it to an auditor.
