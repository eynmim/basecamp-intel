<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — a signature check that skipped a field</b>
A secure-boot check can pass while quietly ignoring part of the image.

CVE-2026-46728 in U-Boot (versions before 2026.04) is a FIT signature bypass: the hashed-nodes field was left out of the hash calculation.

The engineering lesson is that verification is only as strong as what gets hashed. A signature over most of the image still lets an attacker change the rest. I would add a negative test to CI that tampers with every node and expects a boot failure.

Do you test that your secure boot actually rejects a modified image?

<b>2. Post: Tooling unlock — ESP-IDF 6.0 changes the defaults</b>
ESP-IDF 6.0 quietly changes the C library underneath your firmware.

Picolibc is now the default, crypto moves to the PSA Crypto API, legacy drivers are gone and compiler warnings are errors.

If you maintain ESP32-S3 firmware, a clean build on 5.x is no longer a guide to how 6.0 will behave. The warnings-as-errors change alone will surface code that was always slightly wrong.

Which would hurt your codebase most: the libc swap or the removed drivers?

<b>3. Post: Regulatory shift — the CRA standards are still being approved</b>
The EU Cyber Resilience Act is law, but the standards that say how to comply are still in approval.

ETSI published 17 final drafts on 13 August covering product categories that include IoT. Final versions are expected by December 2026, and main obligations apply from December 2027.

For firmware teams this means security design choices, such as update paths and vulnerability handling, are being made before the harmonised standards exist.
