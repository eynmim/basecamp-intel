<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — unplugging is an attack surface</b>
A USB cable being unplugged was enough to leave a dangling pointer in Zephyr.

CVE-2026-10663 describes the USB host stack freeing the root usb_device slab object without clearing the cached ctx->root pointer after a device disconnects. The stack is experimental, so real-world exposure is limited.

What I take from it is that disconnect paths are where lifetime bugs hide. We test the happy path of enumeration, but hot-unplug mid-transfer is when cached pointers outlive their owners.

Do you fuzz your teardown paths as hard as your init paths?

https://t.me/s/cveNotify/196072

<b>3. Post: Tooling unlock — ESP-IDF v6 changes the defaults</b>
ESP-IDF v6.0 changed the C library under your feet.

Picolibc is now the default, alongside a new Installation Manager, a new crypto API layer, compiler warnings treated as errors, and removal of legacy drivers.

For anyone with a long-lived ESP32-S3 codebase, that means a migration to plan, not just a version bump. Warnings-as-errors alone will surface a lot of code that used to pass quietly.

I am planning a test pass of my own firmware on v6.0.x. Have you migrated yet, and what broke first?

<b>4. Post: Regulatory shift — CRA swallows the RED rules</b>
In December 2027 one EU regulation is meant to replace another for connected-product security.

The Cyber Resilience Act applies from 11 December 2027, and the Commission plans to repeal the RED cybersecurity delegated act from that date. CRA Annex I covers the same ground as RED Article 3(3)(d), (e) and (f).

For firmware engineers this means EN 18031 work done today is not wasted, but secure update and vulnerability handling become part of the product, not an afterthought.

Is your team treating the CRA as a legal problem or an engineering one?
