<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — The field you forgot to sign</b>
A signature check that passes when one property is missing is not a signature check.

CVE-2026-46728 affects U-Boot before 2026.04. In FIT image verification, omitting the hashed-nodes property let unauthorised kernels, device trees or ramdisks load.

The insight: verified boot fails at the edges of the format, not in the cryptography. What the verifier treats as optional is what an attacker controls.

Does your bootloader reject images by default, or accept what it cannot check?

<b>2. Post: Regulatory shift — Disclosure is now part of the job</b>
Since 11 September, EU manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities in their products.

That is the CRA reporting duty. The rest of the regulation applies from 11 December 2027, so the process arrives before the full compliance work.

For firmware engineers this makes vulnerability handling a design input: an SBOM, a way to push a patch, and a contact who can answer in hours.

Could your team find every device running a given library version today?

https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting

<b>5. Post: Tooling unlock — Support windows are a design input</b>
Zephyr 4.3 reached end of life on 15 October, and 4.5 is still only planned.

Zephyr ships on a six-month cadence, so a product on an older release can fall out of support before it ships.

Pinning a release is fine; planning the migration date at project start is the part people skip.

When does the RTOS under your current project stop getting fixes?
