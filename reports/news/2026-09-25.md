<b>═ 🔥 TOP STORY ═</b>
A stack-based buffer overflow in Zyxel's GS1900-series smart managed switches (CVE-2026-7273, CVSS 8.8) has been under active exploitation since August 17, and new reporting this week confirms a suspected Chinese-speaking threat actor has compromised 996 switches across 48 countries — with Italy, the US, Taiwan and South Korea among the hardest-hit. The bug sits in the switch's CGI web-management handler: an unauthenticated attacker on the LAN segment sends one crafted HTTP request and executes arbitrary OS commands, no login required. Once in, the actor has been harvesting each switch's configuration, network topology and hashed root-level credentials — reconnaissance that turns a compromised edge switch into a foothold for everything behind it. Zyxel patched the flaw back in June, months before this exploitation wave was detected, which is the uncomfortable part: a fix sitting unapplied on unmonitored network infrastructure is exactly the gap attackers count on. Why it matters: a smart switch is as much an embedded device as any ESP32 board — same class of stack-overflow bug, same unauthenticated-LAN-handler attack surface — and the six-month gap between patch and mass compromise says more about firmware-update discipline than about the bug itself.

<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — 996 switches and a patch nobody installed</b>
996 network switches compromised across 48 countries, and the fix has been sitting in a firmware file since June.

CVE-2026-7273 is a stack-based buffer overflow in Zyxel GS1900 switches, CVSS 8.8. An unauthenticated attacker on the LAN sends one crafted HTTP request to the switch's CGI management handler and gets arbitrary OS command execution — no login needed. A suspected Chinese-speaking actor has been exploiting it since August 17, harvesting configs, topology data and hashed root credentials from nearly 1,000 devices, Italy among the hardest-hit countries alongside the US, Taiwan and South Korea.

The detail that stands out isn't the bug — it's the calendar. Zyxel shipped the patch in June. The mass-exploitation wave was detected months later, on hardware nobody was watching closely enough to update. A fix nobody applies protects nothing.

<b>2. Post: Physics-meets-architecture — sub-50 nanoamps, still doing Bluetooth</b>
Sub-50 nanoamps. That's the hibernation current on a chip that still does Bluetooth LE and 802.15.4.

Nordic's new nRF54LC10A pairs a 128 MHz Cortex-M33 with a RISC-V coprocessor, Bluetooth LE with Channel Sounding, and 802.15.4 for Thread, Zigbee and Matter — and hits a sub-50 nA hibernation mode aimed at devices shipped with the coin-cell battery already installed.

Hibernation current isn't the same problem as sleep current. Sleep current is what a device burns between duty cycles while it's working. Hibernation current is what it burns sitting in a warehouse or on a shelf before it's ever switched on — and that's the number that decides whether a coin-cell product is still alive when the end customer finally opens the box.

Have you actually measured your own design's hibernation draw, or only its active sleep current?

<b>4. Post: Regulatory shift — the EU pushed back its own cybersecurity law's homework</b>
The EU just admitted its own cybersecurity law's homework wasn't going to be ready on time.

A July 2026 European Commission draft amendment pushes back the Cyber Resilience Act's 2026 harmonized-standards drafting deadlines by two months. Those standards — being drafted by CEN, CENELEC and ETSI — are what translate the CRA's legal text into checkable engineering requirements; they're still in drafting and public-comment phase.

Until they land, "presumption of conformity" — the shortcut that lets you certify against a published standard instead of proving compliance from first principles — isn't available yet. Anyone building toward CRA compliance right now is still working from the regulation's raw text, not from an engineering checklist, and that gap just got two months longer.

Is your team building CRA compliance into the design now, or waiting for the standards to land first?
