<b>═ 🔥 TOP STORY ═</b>
Zephyr 4.5.0 was tagged on 28 Oct. It adds Infineon TriCore and Qualcomm Hexagon DSP architectures, an experimental zero-copy media pipeline framework (mpipe), QUIC/HTTP/3, an SSH server and client, new driver APIs (buzzer, clock monitor, DALI, LIN, MUX, pulse IO), SPDX 3 SBOM generation and hardware-accelerated PSA Crypto on selected Silicon Labs, NXP and STM32 parts. It brings 178 new boards and 29 new shields, and is supported until 28 October 2027. Why it matters: the SBOM and PSA Crypto work lines Zephyr up with EU Cyber Resilience Act paperwork, and mpipe is the first serious camera/media plumbing in the kernel tree.

<b>1. Post: Integration leap — a media pipeline inside an RTOS</b>
Zephyr just got a GStreamer-inspired pipeline framework.

Release 4.5.0 adds mpipe, an experimental framework for zero-copy media pipelines. It is marked experimental, so this is a direction, not a finished product.

I have built camera firmware where every frame copy ate into the budget. A shared pipeline abstraction means sensor, processing and output stages could pass buffers by reference instead of each driver inventing its own handoff.

If your MCU handles video, where do your frame copies happen today?

<b>2. Post: Security wake-up — a length check in a BLE parser</b>
One of the fixes in ESP-IDF v5.5.6 is a buffer-length check in L2CAP parsing to prevent out-of-bounds access.

Small diff, big lesson: the Bluetooth stack parses bytes from anyone in radio range, before any pairing or trust exists.

That makes protocol parsers the part of a BLE product I would fuzz first, ahead of the application logic that sits on top.

Do you fuzz your BLE parsing layer, or only your own code?

https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/releases

<b>3. Post: Capability unlock — one RTOS, DSPs and TriCore</b>
Zephyr 4.5.0 adds two architectures that do not look like typical Cortex-M territory: Infineon TriCore and Qualcomm Hexagon DSP.

That widens the question of what "an embedded RTOS" covers, from sensor nodes toward automotive-style cores and DSP offload.

For a developer, the payoff is one driver model, one build system and one test flow across very different silicon. Whether the ports are mature is something to check board by board.

<b>4. Post: Tooling unlock — SBOMs from the build</b>
Zephyr 4.5 can generate SPDX 3 SBOMs and ships a revamped hardening tool.

In practice an SBOM used to be a spreadsheet someone built before an audit. Generating it from the build means it stays true to the binary you actually ship.

With EU cyber-resilience rules heading to full application on 11 Dec 2027, that is the kind of boring tooling that quietly saves a quarter of work.

Is your SBOM produced by CI, or by a person?

<b>5. Post: Process shift — your RTOS version is a maintenance plan</b>
Zephyr 4.5 is supported until 28 October 2027. 4.4 runs until 12 April 2027.

Twice-yearly releases mean the support window for any product starts ticking the day you pick a version.

I would pick the version, write down who owns the next upgrade, and schedule the rebuild before the old branch expires, not after.
