<b>1. Post: Security wake-up — a filesystem library with no patch is already inside your SD card slot</b>
Seven bugs, one of them CVSS 7.6, and only one has an upstream fix. The rest are still open in a library that's probably in your bill of materials right now.

runZero disclosed seven vulnerabilities in FatFs, the FAT/exFAT library bundled into a huge share of embedded firmware for reading SD cards and USB drives. The worst, an integer overflow during FAT32 mounting, gives an attacker memory corruption and code execution from nothing more than a crafted SD card image. FreeRTOS+FAT and most vendor HAL wrappers (STM32, ESP-IDF) build directly on the same core.

The part worth sitting with: most MCUs have no ASLR, no stack canaries, no modern heap protections. A bug that would be "just a crash" on a desktop OS is a clean code-execution primitive on the class of device this library actually ships on.

If your firmware mounts an SD card or USB drive, do you know which FatFs version it's running?

<b>4. Post: Tooling unlock — your BLE scanner was probably missing 90% of advertisements and you'd never know</b>
No error message. No crash. Just quietly worse data for two months.

Since ESP-IDF 2026.7.1, a Wi-Fi/BLE coexistence bug meant a configured 30 ms scan window inside a 320 ms interval was honored only 9.4% of the time whenever Wi-Fi was active — BLE trackers and proxies were silently missing most advertisements. ESP-IDF 5.5.5 fixes the coexistence timing, and Espressif's new guidance is to set the scan window equal to the interval.

The lesson generalizes past this one bug: coexistence bugs between radios sharing the same RF front-end are some of the hardest to catch in testing, because nothing fails loudly — your scan just quietly gets worse, and every symptom looks like "the wireless environment," not "the SDK."

If your ESP32-S3 project scans BLE while Wi-Fi is active, have you actually measured your real scan window lately?

<b>5. Post: Regulatory shift — Sep 11 is when the EU starts timing your vulnerability disclosures in hours, not quarters</b>
Compliance deadlines usually feel abstract until you notice the clock they start. This one starts a 24-hour clock.

From September 11, 2026, the EU Cyber Resilience Act requires manufacturers to report actively exploited vulnerabilities within 24 hours, a detailed report within 72 hours, and a final report within 14 days. It lands right after the Aug 30 deadline for the first batch of harmonized "Principles of Cyber Resilience" standards under CRA Annex I Part 1.

The detail worth internalizing if you ship or plan to ship embedded products into the EU: this isn't a paperwork requirement you can absorb after the fact. A 24-hour first-report clock means the incident-response pipeline (who gets notified, what gets logged, who drafts the report) has to already exist before the first exploited CVE shows up, not be built in response to one.

If you were shipping a product into the EU today, could your team actually hit that 24-hour window?
